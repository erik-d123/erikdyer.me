# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Recruiters, research collaborators, engineers, professors, and classmates who want a fast, credible read on Erik Dyer's technical interests and trajectory. They are likely skimming between tabs, often on a laptop, and need to understand what he builds, where he has worked, what research lane he occupies, and how to contact him.

## Product Purpose

This site is a personal portfolio and credibility surface. It should make Erik legible as a Princeton ECE student working across AI systems, infrastructure, agents, reinforcement learning, and 3D/spatial computing. Success means a visitor can understand the through-line in under a minute, then find concrete proof points without encountering fake projects, placeholders, or forced metaphors.

## Brand Personality

Rigorous, systems-minded, precise. The voice should feel like a field notebook for technical work: concise, evidence-oriented, and distinctive without theatricality. It should suggest taste and curiosity, but it should not overclaim.

## Anti-references

Do not use forced neuroscience or biology metaphors unless they are directly relevant to the work. Avoid generic AI founder landing pages, startup hero sections, decorative SaaS gradients, fake product names, inflated metrics, and placeholder links. Avoid making coursework or private research sound like shipped public software.

## Design Principles

1. Lead with the through-line: AI systems that need evaluation, infrastructure, and real-world constraints.
2. Show proof before personality: concrete research, work, and project descriptions should carry the site.
3. Be precise about status: public, private, research, coursework, and internship work should be clearly distinguishable.
4. Keep the notebook feel, remove the costume: diagrams, labels, and chronology can stay if they support systems work.
5. Make contact and next steps obvious: resume, email, GitHub, and LinkedIn should be easy to find and functional.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Aim for a strong public-portfolio baseline: readable contrast, keyboard-accessible navigation, visible focus states, useful alt text, reduced-motion support where animation exists, and no horizontal scrolling on common mobile or desktop viewports.
